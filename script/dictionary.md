# Dictionary — Twerp's dictionary pages

The dictionary works on every page. Its corner appears when the newsletter pop-up closes (`newsletter.md`), and
words stay unlocked for the browser tab. `## (cover)` is the page shown before any word is unlocked.

Each `## word` is one page. A page only shows up (as a tab) once the story adds it with `## Dictionary add`
(in `scene3a.md` / `scene3b.md`), or with `dictionary:` on a `## Hotspot`.
`[^6]` in a definition is a footnote that links to note 6 on the Notes page (`notes.md`).

Where each word is added:
- local: 3.3a, first dictionary call
- civic nationalism, ethnic nationalism, ethno-symbolism: 3.3a, joint plot
- cult of the fallen soldier: 3.3a, after RV's Mosse line
- heritage: 3.3a, "Flip the page"
- imagined community: 3.3b, the "person" hotspot
- Gothic Revival: the walk, Unit B (so the dictionary can first appear on the walk)
- consciousness: Scene 1, when the reader sees the hijacked-ad Marx quote (and again in the 3.3b sermon notes,
  if they haven't got it yet)
- transitional social reality: 3.3b, during the sermon notes
- banal nationalism, narration (of the nation): written but NOT added anywhere yet (the design doc marks them optional;
  the dictionary only exists on the Scene 3 page, so they can't be added in Scene 1, 2 or 4 without more work).
- `pronunciation`, `pos` (part of speech) and `source` (shown small at the bottom) are optional.
- `cite` awards that sticker (see `stickers.md`) when the page is shown. `local` and `heritage` award the
  `dictionary` sticker, whose citation is a placeholder until you add the real dictionary.
- The body is the definition. Numbered senses work best as separate paragraphs.

The definitions below are PLACEHOLDERS written in plain language. Replace them with the entry from the
dictionary you're citing (and put the citation in `source`).

## local

**1.** *adj.* Belonging to or connected with a particular place or area, especially the one you are in.

**2.** *adj.* Relating to a small area rather than a whole country or the world.

**3.** *n.* A person who lives in a particular place, as opposed to a visitor.

## heritage

pos: noun

**1.** Property, traditions or characteristics that are passed down from earlier generations.

**2.** Buildings, objects and practices from the past that a community treats as valuable and worth keeping.

## civic nationalism
cite: smith
pos: noun

Belonging to a nation through shared commitment to its laws, institutions and values, open in principle to anyone who joins (Smith).[^6]

## ethnic nationalism
cite: smith
pos: noun

Belonging to a nation through shared descent, language or culture: something you are born into rather than choose (Smith).[^6]

## ethno-symbolism
cite: smith
pos: noun

Anthony D. Smith's view that nations are built from older myths, memories, symbols and values, which give a nation its sense of continuity with the past.[^6]

## cult of the fallen soldier
cite: mosse
pos: noun

George L. Mosse's term for how war memorials after 1918 turned soldiers' deaths into a sacred sacrifice for the nation, borrowing Christian imagery.[^9]

## banal nationalism
cite: billig
pos: noun

Michael Billig's term for national identity reproduced through everyday, unnoticed reminders, like the flag hanging on a public building rather than the one being waved.[^4]

## narration (of the nation)
cite: bhabha
pos: noun

Homi K. Bhabha's idea that a nation exists as a story told by many conflicting voices, split between a *pedagogical* official history and a *performative* everyday life.[^5]

## imagined community
cite: anderson
pos: noun

Benedict Anderson's definition of the nation: people who will never meet most of their fellow members, yet imagine themselves as one community.[^11]

## consciousness
pronunciation: /ˈkɒn.ʃəs.nəs/
pos: noun

Awareness of oneself and the world.

For Marx, something made by the way people live: "it is conversely their social being which determines their consciousness."

In the sermon, the self that everything you make comes out of.

In the notes, the categories we understand ourselves through.

## transitional social reality
cite: bhabha
pos: noun phrase

Bhabha's phrase for how a nation actually lives: always in passage, never settled into its claimed "origins."[^12]

## Gothic Revival
cite: lewis
pos: noun

The nineteenth-century return to medieval Gothic architecture, which in Germany became a contested symbol of national, regional and religious identity (Lewis).[^13]
